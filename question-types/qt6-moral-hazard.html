Question Types: Moral Hazard

All Core Microeconomics finals questions on moral hazard / principal-agent problems from 2014 to 2025, organised by Part A short problem, Part B essay, and Part B long-answer problem. Maps to Topic 8 / Information Economics 2 in the 2025 reading list.
Moral hazard is the most frequently tested Part B Long Answer topic, appearing in 2014, 2017, and 2024. Master the canonical principal-agent setup with two states, two effort levels and sqrt utility, since it underlies most variants.

Excluded sub-topics

The Oxford syllabus does not test:

  • Multi-period principal-agent contracts with renegotiation.
  • Career concerns models (Holmstrom 1982) as a separate question family.
  • Tournament theory or relative performance evaluation.
  • Multitask principal-agent (Holmstrom-Milgrom 1991 multitask paper) beyond the linear single-task case in 2014 Q11.
  • Common agency or multi-principal models.

The 2014 to 2025 papers stay within: two effort levels (sometimes three), two outcomes (sometimes three), sqrt or CARA utility, and the binding IR / IC pair. The Peltzman essay is the one applied variant tested.

Part A: short compulsory problems

Sub-question Type A.1: First-best contract when effort is observable

  • What it asks: Risk-neutral principal hires risk-averse agent with utility $v(w) = \sqrt{w}$ (or log) and reservation utility $\bar u$. Effort $e$ is observable and contractible. Find the wage that implements a given effort level.
  • Step-by-step guide:
  • Observation point: when effort is observable the principal writes a forcing contract, "pay $w^*$ if you choose $e$, pay zero (or a punishment) otherwise". The IC constraint is slack.
  • Risk-sharing principle: a risk-neutral principal and risk-averse agent share risk efficiently when the agent bears no risk. So pay a constant wage in every state.
  • Bind IR: the principal pays the lowest constant wage that meets participation. With $v(w) = \sqrt{w}$ and effort cost $c(e)$:

$$\sqrt{w^} - c(e) = \bar u \implies w^ = (\bar u + c(e))^2.$$

  1. Compute principal's expected profit: $\Pi^{FB}(e) = \pi_L(e) x_L + \pi_H(e) x_H - w^*(e)$.
  2. Pick optimal effort: principal picks the $e$ that maximises $\Pi^{FB}(e)$ across the allowed effort levels.
  3. Appeared in: 2015 Q3(a), 2019 Q4(a), 2023 Q3(a), 2024 Q10(a) (low-effort baseline).
  4. Relevant concepts: Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem, Concepts/First-Best Contract, Concepts/Individual Rationality, Concepts/Risk vs Insurance Trade-off.

    5. Sub-question Type A.2: Second-best contract under unobservable effort (binary effort, binary outcome)

    • What it asks: Same setup as A.1 but effort is unobservable. Find the wage schedule $(w_L, w_H)$ that implements high effort at minimum expected cost.
    • Step-by-step guide:
    • State the problem: $\min_{w_L, w_H} \pi_L(e_H) w_L + \pi_H(e_H) w_H$ subject to IR and IC.
    • IR (individual rationality):

    $$\pi_L(e_H) \sqrt{w_L} + \pi_H(e_H) \sqrt{w_H} - c(e_H) \geq \bar u.$$

    1. IC (incentive compatibility, prefers $e_H$ over $e_L$):

      2. $$\pi_L(e_H) \sqrt{w_L} + \pi_H(e_H) \sqrt{w_H} - c(e_H) \geq \pi_L(e_L) \sqrt{w_L} + \pi_H(e_L) \sqrt{w_H} - c(e_L).$$

      1. Both bind at the optimum: a standard result. If IR were slack, lower both wages a touch. If IC were slack, lower $w_H$ and raise $w_L$ to reduce wage risk.
      2. Solve the linear system in $\sqrt{w_L}, \sqrt{w_H}$: substitute $u_L = \sqrt{w_L}, u_H = \sqrt{w_H}$. The two equations are linear in $(u_L, u_H)$:

        3. $$\pi_L(e_H) u_L + \pi_H(e_H) u_H = \bar u + c(e_H),$$

        $$[\pi_H(e_H) - \pi_H(e_L)](u_H - u_L) = c(e_H) - c(e_L).$$

        1. Closed form:

          2. $$u_H - u_L = \frac{c(e_H) - c(e_L)}{\pi_H(e_H) - \pi_H(e_L)} > 0,$$

          which gives the wage spread, then plug into IR to pin down $u_L$.

          1. Square to recover wages: $w_L = u_L^2, w_H = u_H^2$.
          2. Appeared in: 2015 Q3(b), 2019 Q4(b), 2023 Q3(c), 2024 Q10(c, d) (with wage bounds).
          3. Relevant concepts: Concepts/Second-Best Contract, Concepts/Incentive Compatibility, Concepts/Individual Rationality, Concepts/Moral Hazard.

            4. Sub-question Type A.3: Check incentive compatibility of a proposed contract

            • What it asks: A specific wage schedule (often a revenue-sharing contract or a candidate $(w_L, w_H)$) is given. Show whether the agent prefers high effort or low effort and whether the contract satisfies IR.
            • Step-by-step guide:
            • Compute expected utility under each effort:

            $$EU(e) = \pi_L(e) \sqrt{w_L} + \pi_H(e) \sqrt{w_H} - c(e).$$

            1. IC check: high effort is IC iff $EU(e_H) \geq EU(e_L)$. Often the question gives numerical $\pi$'s and wages, so it is a direct substitution.
            2. IR check: compare $EU(e^) \geq \bar u$ where $e^$ is the effort the agent will actually pick (the one with higher $EU$).
            3. State the conclusion crisply: does the contract implement high effort, and at what expected cost to the principal?
            4. Appeared in: 2019 Q4(b) (given $w_L = 36$, find $w_H$ binding PC, check IC), 2023 Q3(b) (check revenue-sharing 30%, 6% contract).
            5. Relevant concepts: Concepts/Incentive Compatibility, Concepts/Individual Rationality, Concepts/Revenue Sharing.

              6. Sub-question Type A.4: Compute the agency cost

              • What it asks: Compare the principal's expected profit under the first-best (observable effort) contract and the second-best (unobservable effort) contract that implements the same effort level. The difference is the agency cost.
              • Step-by-step guide:
              • Compute $w^{FB}$: from A.1, $w^{FB} = (\bar u + c(e_H))^2$.
              • Compute expected second-best wage bill: $E[w^{SB}] = \pi_L(e_H) w_L^{SB} + \pi_H(e_H) w_H^{SB}$.
              • By Jensen's inequality: since $\sqrt{\cdot}$ is concave and the agent gets the same expected utility in both cases, $E[w^{SB}] > w^{FB}$. The agent receives a risk premium for bearing wage risk.
              • Agency cost = $E[w^{SB}] - w^{FB}$, or equivalently the difference in principal's expected profit between the two regimes.
              • Interpretation: agency cost is the welfare loss from the principal needing to use wage variation to incentivise effort, since the agent now bears risk that he would rather not bear.
              • Appeared in: 2015 Q3(c), 2019 Q4(c), 2023 Q3(d).
              • Relevant concepts: Concepts/Agency Cost, Concepts/Risk vs Insurance Trade-off, Concepts/Second-Best Contract.

              Sub-question Type A.5: Revenue-sharing contract design

              • What it asks: Restrict attention to contracts of the form $w = \alpha \cdot$ (revenue) or $w = t + s x$. Find the share $s$ (or $\alpha$) that implements the desired effort at minimum cost subject to IR.
              • Step-by-step guide:
              • Compute the agent's IC under share $s$: $EU(e_H, s) \geq EU(e_L, s)$. With binary outcomes this gives a lower bound $s \geq \underline s$.
              • Compute the agent's IR under share $s$: $EU(e_H, s) \geq \bar u$. If a fixed component $t$ is allowed, set $t$ to bind IR.
              • Principal's cost: minimised by setting $s$ at the IC lower bound when the principal wants to implement $e_H$.
              • Check the constraint set: if the question forces a pure revenue-share with no fixed component, IR may bind below the IC bound, in which case the share must be raised further.
              • Appeared in: 2023 Q3(b, c).
              • Relevant concepts: Concepts/Revenue Sharing, Concepts/Incentive Compatibility, Concepts/Linear Contracts.

              Sub-question Type A.6: Implementing low effort when effort is unobservable

              • What it asks: If the principal wants to induce $e_L$ (the cheapest effort) under hidden action, what is the optimal contract?
              • Step-by-step guide:
              • Observation point: to implement $e_L$ no IC constraint binds in the "downward" direction since $e_L$ is already the cheapest action. The only binding constraint is IR.
              • Constant wage: pay a constant wage $w^L = (\bar u + c(e_L))^2$, the same as first-best.
              • No risk premium: the agent bears no risk and the principal extracts all surplus. Implementing low effort is therefore the same cost as the first-best for low effort.
              • Compare with high-effort second-best: the agency cost only appears when the principal wants to implement high effort.
              • Appeared in: 2015 Q3(a), 2024 Q10(a).
              • Relevant concepts: Concepts/First-Best Contract, Concepts/Individual Rationality, Concepts/Moral Hazard.

              Sub-question Type A.7: Two-effort two-state canonical setup, full walkthrough

              • What it asks: Combination of A.1, A.2, A.4 in one question. Often phrased "compare implementing $e=0$ vs $e=1$ and find the optimum".
              • Step-by-step guide:
              • Step 1: principal's outside benchmark: write down expected profit under both effort levels, ignoring contract structure.
              • Step 2: cost of implementing each effort:
              • $e_L$: use A.6, constant wage.
              • $e_H$: use A.2, solve the two-equation IR/IC linear system in $\sqrt{w_L}, \sqrt{w_H}$.
              • Step 3: net profit comparison: pick the effort that gives higher expected profit net of wage costs.
              • Step 4: report the optimal contract as well as the agency cost.
              • Appeared in: 2015 Q3, 2019 Q4, 2023 Q3.
              • Relevant concepts: Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem, Concepts/First-Best Contract, Concepts/Second-Best Contract, Concepts/Agency Cost.

              Part B: essay-style questions

              Sub-question Type B.E.1: Evaluate statements on the principal-agent problem (2022 Q9)

              • What it asks: A two-profit, two-effort principal-agent setup with risk-averse agent. Three statements are given, often along the lines of "(i) the agent always bears risk, (ii) the agency cost rises in the agent's risk aversion, (iii) if the principal is also risk-averse, the contract becomes first-best." The candidate must evaluate each.
              • Step-by-step guide for a top essay:
              • Intro thesis: set up the canonical model in one paragraph. Two outcomes $x_L < x_H$, two efforts $e_L < e_H$ with $\pi_H(e_H) > \pi_H(e_L)$. Risk-neutral principal, risk-averse agent with $v(w) = \sqrt{w}$, effort cost $c(\cdot)$, reservation utility $\bar u$. State that the IC and IR constraints both bind when the principal wants to implement $e_H$.
              • Statement (i) "the agent must bear wage risk to provide incentives": true when implementing $e_H$ under unobservable effort, since the IC constraint requires $w_H > w_L$. False when implementing $e_L$ since constant wage works. Also false in the first-best.
              • Statement (ii) "agency cost rises in risk aversion": true. With a more concave utility (say $v(w) = w^{1/\rho}$ with higher $\rho$), the agent demands a larger risk premium for the same wage spread, so the principal pays more in expectation. Formally, the Jensen gap between $E[w]$ and $w^{FB}$ widens.
              • Statement (iii) "if principal is risk-averse too, contract becomes first-best": false in general. The principal becoming risk-averse changes the trade-off but does not remove the IC constraint, which is the source of the second-best inefficiency. What changes is risk-sharing: optimal contracts shift some risk back to the principal.
              • Conclusion: tie together. The friction is hidden action plus risk aversion together. Either alone is harmless: observable effort gives first-best regardless of risk aversion, and a risk-neutral agent gives first-best via "sell the firm to the agent".
              • Appeared in: 2022 Q9.
              • Relevant concepts: Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem, Concepts/Agency Cost, Concepts/Risk vs Insurance Trade-off, Concepts/Moral Hazard.

              Sub-question Type B.E.2: Peltzman effect essay (2023 Q9)

              • What it asks: "Does a safer-looking road make drivers safer?" Apply moral hazard theory to behavioural risk compensation.
              • Step-by-step guide for a top essay:
              • Intro thesis: Peltzman (1975) argued that safety improvements induce more risky behaviour, so the net effect on accidents is ambiguous. Frame it as a hidden-action problem: society wants safe driving but cannot observe each driver's effort.
              • Model setup: driver chooses driving care $e$ at private cost $c(e)$. Probability of accident is $\pi(e, q)$ where $q$ is road safety. Assume $\partial \pi / \partial e < 0$ (more care reduces accidents) and $\partial \pi / \partial q < 0$.
              • Crucial cross-partial: $\partial^2 \pi / \partial e \partial q$. If safer roads reduce the marginal benefit of care (so the cross-partial is positive in absolute terms, meaning care matters less when the road is safer), the driver optimally lowers $e$. Algebraically, the driver's FOC $\partial \pi / \partial e = -c'(e) / (\text{cost of accident})$ shifts.
              • Empirical interlude: cite Peltzman's seatbelt analysis (mixed evidence). Modern evidence on anti-lock brakes, helmet laws, condoms in HIV reduction, similar effects. The empirical magnitude varies, but the qualitative direction (some offsetting) is widely accepted.
              • Welfare implications: even with full risk compensation the policy can be welfare-improving if accident severity falls (drivers crash at similar rates but suffer less harm). Distinguish accident frequency from accident damage.
              • Policy response: pair infrastructure improvements with rule changes that re-impose driver risk (lower speed limits, stricter penalties, mandatory insurance with copay). Insurance design with copay is the principal-agent solution.
              • Conclusion: Peltzman effects are real but rarely large enough to fully offset the safety gain. The policy implication is to bundle physical safety with incentive design.
              • Appeared in: 2023 Q9.
              • Relevant concepts: Concepts/Peltzman Effect, Concepts/Moral Hazard, Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem.

              Sub-question Type B.E.3: Adverse selection vs moral hazard under symmetric/asymmetric info (2024 Q9)

              • What it asks: "Adverse selection and moral hazard are equally relevant under asymmetric information and equally irrelevant under symmetric information." Evaluate.
              • Step-by-step guide for a top essay (MH side, since AS lives in the AS file):
              • Intro thesis: agree with the broad framing but qualify. Asymmetric information about types (AS) and asymmetric information about actions (MH) both vanish under symmetric information, but they cause different distortions and call for different remedies.
              • Define MH carefully: hidden action after the contract is signed. Examples: worker shirking, insured driving recklessly, fund manager over-trading.
              • Under symmetric info: if effort were observable the principal writes a forcing contract, IC is slack, and the first-best is achieved. So moral hazard is irrelevant under symmetric information. Same conclusion holds for AS under symmetric type information.
              • Under asymmetric info: both bite, but the distortions differ. MH causes risk-averse agents to bear wage risk to satisfy IC, giving the agency cost. AS causes separation via screening that distorts the low-type's coverage (Rothschild-Stiglitz) or full pooling collapse (Akerlof). The remedies are different too: signalling and screening for AS, output-contingent contracts and bonding for MH.
              • "Equally relevant" qualification: in some real markets they coexist (insurance is both). In others one dominates: managerial compensation is mostly MH, used cars are mostly AS.
              • Conclusion: the statement gets the symmetric-info half right (both irrelevant). The asymmetric-info half is partly right (both can bite) but misleading: they need different cures.
              • Appeared in: 2024 Q9 (MH side, cross-listed; the AS side is covered in the Adverse Selection file).
              • Relevant concepts: Concepts/Moral Hazard, Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem, Concepts/First-Best Contract, Concepts/Second-Best Contract.

              Sub-question Type B.E.4: College hires risk-averse real estate agent (2025 Q9)

              • What it asks: A college hires a real estate agent to sell a property. The agent is risk-averse. Why does the fee depend on the selling price? How do increases in the agent's reservation utility, or in the agent's risk aversion, affect IR and IC and the optimal contract?
              • Step-by-step guide for a top essay:
              • Intro thesis: a commission tied to the selling price is the standard solution to a hidden-effort problem. The agent chooses unobservable effort (showings, marketing, negotiation) that raises the expected sale price. A flat fee gives zero incentive; a pure-commission fee imposes too much risk on the agent. The optimal contract balances incentives and insurance.
              • Model: let sale price $x$ have distribution $F(x|e)$ depending on effort $e$. Agent has utility $v(w) - c(e)$ where $v$ is concave. Principal (college) is risk-neutral.
              • Why fee depends on price: by the IC argument from B.L.1, implementing $e_H$ requires $w(x_H) > w(x_L)$. The slope of $w(x)$ is the incentive intensity. A flat fee makes IC fail and the agent shirks.
              • Effect of higher reservation utility $\bar u$:
              • IR shifts up. The agent demands more total expected pay.
              • The principal scales the entire wage schedule up (intuitively, add a constant to $w(x)$). The slope $w'(x)$ that satisfies IC is unchanged because IC is a difference condition.
              • In the linear-contract version (2014 Q11), $\bar u$ enters $t$ but not $s^* = 1/(1 + r \sigma^2 c)$.
              • The principal's expected profit falls one-for-one with the rise in $\bar u$ (above some threshold the principal would not hire at all).
              • Effect of higher risk aversion $r$:
              • IR is tightened for any wage schedule with variance, since the agent demands a larger risk premium.
              • IC is unchanged in the binary-state model (it is a comparison of expected utilities under two effort distributions), but in the mean-variance / CARA model the wage variance enters both IR and IC.
              • Optimal slope $s^*$ falls (less incentive, more insurance), so effort falls. The agency cost rises. The principal may switch to implementing $e_L$ if $r$ is high enough.
              • Optimal contract amendment: practical implication is that the commission rate should be lower for more risk-averse agents (in the limit, a flat fee for a very risk-averse agent makes sense even though effort drops to the minimum). The college also benefits from selecting agents who are less risk-averse, since contracting with them is cheaper.
              • Conclusion: the dependence of fee on sale price is a moral hazard remedy. The parameters $\bar u$ and $r$ affect IR and IC differently: $\bar u$ shifts the level, $r$ shifts the slope.
              • Appeared in: 2025 Q9.
              • Relevant concepts: Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem, Concepts/Individual Rationality, Concepts/Incentive Compatibility, Concepts/Linear Contracts, Concepts/CARA Utility, Concepts/Risk vs Insurance Trade-off.

              Part B: long-answer problems

              Sub-question Type B.L.1: Two-state principal-agent with two effort levels (canonical)

              • What it asks: Risk-neutral principal hires risk-averse agent with $v(w) = \sqrt{w}$. Two outcomes $x_L, x_H$ with probabilities depending on effort. Two effort levels $e_L < e_H$ with costs $c_L < c_H$. Reservation utility $\bar u$. Find first-best and second-best contracts, the principal's profit under each, and the agency cost.
              • Step-by-step guide:
              • Set up notation: wages $w_L, w_H$ in low and high outcome, utility $v(w) = \sqrt{w}$, effort cost $c(e)$, probabilities $p_L(e), p_H(e) = 1 - p_L(e)$.
              • First-best (observable effort): principal pays a constant wage $w^$ such that $\sqrt{w^} = \bar u + c(e)$, so $w^ = (\bar u + c(e))^2$. The principal picks the effort that maximises $\pi_L(e) x_L + \pi_H(e) x_H - w^(e)$. The agent bears no risk, full risk-sharing is achieved.
              • Second-best (unobservable effort): principal solves

              $$\min_{w_L, w_H} \pi_L(e_H) w_L + \pi_H(e_H) w_H$$

              subject to IR: $\pi_L(e_H) \sqrt{w_L} + \pi_H(e_H) \sqrt{w_H} - c(e_H) \geq \bar u$, and IC: $\pi_L(e_H) \sqrt{w_L} + \pi_H(e_H) \sqrt{w_H} - c(e_H) \geq \pi_L(e_L) \sqrt{w_L} + \pi_H(e_L) \sqrt{w_H} - c(e_L)$.

              1. Lagrangian: $L = \pi_L^H w_L + \pi_H^H w_H - \lambda [\pi_L^H \sqrt{w_L} + \pi_H^H \sqrt{w_H} - c_H - \bar u] - \mu [(\pi_L^H - \pi_L^L) \sqrt{w_L} + (\pi_H^H - \pi_H^L) \sqrt{w_H} - (c_H - c_L)]$, where I write $\pi_s^e = \pi_s(e)$ for brevity.
              2. FOCs: $\partial L / \partial w_L = \pi_L^H - \lambda \pi_L^H \cdot \frac{1}{2\sqrt{w_L}} - \mu (\pi_L^H - \pi_L^L) \cdot \frac{1}{2\sqrt{w_L}} = 0$, and analogously for $w_H$. Rearrange:

                3. $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{w_s}} = \frac{1}{\lambda + \mu \cdot (1 - \pi_s^L / \pi_s^H)} \cdot \frac{2}{1}$$

                after dividing by $\pi_s^H$. The key takeaway: $\sqrt{w_s}$ is a decreasing function of the likelihood ratio $\pi_s^L / \pi_s^H$ (Mirrlees-Holmstrom sufficient statistic).

                1. Linear system shortcut: rather than chase Lagrange multipliers, use that both constraints bind. Let $u_s = \sqrt{w_s}$. The two constraints are linear in $(u_L, u_H)$:

                  2. $$\pi_L^H u_L + \pi_H^H u_H = \bar u + c_H, \qquad (\pi_H^H - \pi_H^L)(u_H - u_L) = c_H - c_L.$$

                  1. Closed form for the spread:

                    2. $$u_H - u_L = \frac{c_H - c_L}{\pi_H^H - \pi_H^L} \equiv \Delta.$$

                    This is the "incentive spread". It rises in the cost gap $c_H - c_L$ and falls in the informativeness of the outcome about effort $\pi_H^H - \pi_H^L$.

                    1. Solve for individual wages: from IR, $u_L = \bar u + c_H - \pi_H^H \cdot \Delta$ and $u_H = u_L + \Delta$. Then $w_s = u_s^2$.
                    2. Expected wage bill under second-best: $E[w^{SB}] = \pi_L^H u_L^2 + \pi_H^H u_H^2$. By Jensen, since $u_L \neq u_H$, $E[w^{SB}] > (E[u])^2 = (\bar u + c_H)^2 = w^{FB}(e_H)$.
                    3. Agency cost: $AC = E[w^{SB}] - w^{FB}(e_H) > 0$. The strict inequality comes from the agent's concave utility times the wage spread $\Delta$.
                    4. Pick optimal effort under second-best: compare net profit of implementing $e_H$ (= $\pi_L^H x_L + \pi_H^H x_H - E[w^{SB}]$) against $e_L$ (= $\pi_L^L x_L + \pi_H^L x_H - (\bar u + c_L)^2$). The principal picks whichever is larger.
                    5. Possible reversal: if the agency cost is large enough, the principal switches from $e_H$ under first-best to $e_L$ under second-best. This is "effort distortion" on top of the risk-sharing distortion.
                    6. Appeared in: 2015 Q3 (Part A version), 2019 Q4 (Part A), 2023 Q3 (Part A), 2017 Q10 (Part B Long, three effort levels), 2024 Q10 (Part B Long, three outcomes with wage bounds).
                    7. Relevant concepts: Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem, Concepts/First-Best Contract, Concepts/Second-Best Contract, Concepts/Individual Rationality, Concepts/Incentive Compatibility, Concepts/Agency Cost, Concepts/Moral Hazard, Concepts/Risk vs Insurance Trade-off.

                      8. Sub-question Type B.L.2: Linear compensation contract in mean-variance setting (Holmstrom-Milgrom)

                      • What it asks: 2014 Q11 setup with $w = t + s x$, agent has CARA utility with coefficient $r$, effort cost $c e^2 / 2$, output $x = e + \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$.
                      • Step-by-step guide:
                      • Why a linear contract? Under CARA + normal noise the certainty equivalent collapses to mean minus variance times $r/2$, which makes the optimum linear (Holmstrom-Milgrom 1987). Restricting to linear without loss of generality.
                      • Certainty equivalent: agent's wage is $w = t + s x = t + s(e + \varepsilon)$, which is $N(t + se, s^2 \sigma^2)$. The CARA certainty equivalent of a normal random wage minus effort cost is:

                      $$CE = t + s e - \frac{r s^2 \sigma^2}{2} - \frac{c e^2}{2}.$$

                      1. Optimal effort given $s$: maximise CE over $e$. FOC: $s = c e$, so $e^* = s / c$. So the slope $s$ is exactly the incentive intensity: doubling $s$ doubles effort.
                      2. Principal's problem: maximise $E[x - w] = e - t - s e = (1 - s) e - t$ subject to IR: $t + s e^ - r s^2 \sigma^2 / 2 - c (e^)^2 / 2 \geq \bar u$.
                      3. Substitute $e^* = s/c$ into IR: $t = \bar u - s \cdot (s/c) + r s^2 \sigma^2 / 2 + c (s/c)^2 / 2 = \bar u - s^2/c + r s^2 \sigma^2 / 2 + s^2 / (2c)$.

                        4. Simplify: $t = \bar u - s^2 / (2c) + r s^2 \sigma^2 / 2$.

                        1. Substitute back into principal's objective: $\Pi = (1 - s) \cdot (s/c) - t = s/c - s^2/c - \bar u + s^2/(2c) - r s^2 \sigma^2 / 2 = s/c - s^2/(2c) - r s^2 \sigma^2 / 2 - \bar u$.
                        2. FOC over $s$: $\partial \Pi / \partial s = 1/c - s/c - r s \sigma^2 = 0$, so $1/c = s (1/c + r \sigma^2)$, giving:

                          3. $$s^* = \frac{1/c}{1/c + r \sigma^2} = \frac{1}{1 + r \sigma^2 c}.$$

                          1. Comparative statics: higher $r$ (more risk-averse agent), higher $\sigma^2$ (noisier output), or higher $c$ (steeper effort cost) all reduce $s^*$. Intuition: with more risk or higher effort cost the trade-off shifts toward insurance over incentives. The agent's effort responds less to incentives, so loading risk on him buys less effort.
                          2. First-best benchmark: when effort is observable, the principal forces $e^{FB}$ via a constant wage. The principal's problem is $\max_e e - c e^2 / 2$, so $e^{FB} = 1/c$. Compare $e^{SB} = s^* / c = 1 / (c + r \sigma^2 c^2) < 1/c = e^{FB}$. Effort is distorted downward.
                          3. First-best welfare: $\Pi^{FB} = 1/(2c) - \bar u$. Second-best welfare: $\Pi^{SB} = s^/c - s^{2}/(2c) - r s^{2} \sigma^2 / 2 - \bar u$. Plug in $s^ = 1/(1 + r \sigma^2 c)$ and simplify: $\Pi^{SB} = \frac{1}{2c(1 + r \sigma^2 c)} - \bar u$.
                          4. Agency cost in mean-variance form: $AC = \Pi^{FB} - \Pi^{SB} = \frac{1}{2c} - \frac{1}{2c(1 + r \sigma^2 c)} = \frac{r \sigma^2}{2(1 + r \sigma^2 c)} > 0$. Rises in $r$ and $\sigma^2$, with the obvious sign.
                          5. Sell-the-firm corner: if $r = 0$ (risk-neutral agent) then $s^ = 1$ and $e^ = 1/c = e^{FB}$, so first-best is reached by selling the firm to the agent. This is a classic result.
                          6. Appeared in: 2014 Q11.
                          7. Relevant concepts: Concepts/Linear Contracts, Concepts/CARA Utility, Concepts/Certainty Equivalent, Concepts/Risk vs Insurance Trade-off, Concepts/Agency Cost, Concepts/First-Best Contract.

                            8. Sub-question Type B.L.3: Three effort levels, binary outcome (Mirrlees / monotone likelihood)

                            • What it asks: 2017 Q10 setup. Risk-neutral farm owner hires risk-averse worker with $v(w) = \sqrt{w}$. Three effort levels $e_L, e_M, e_H$ with probabilities of a good harvest $\pi_H(e_L) < \pi_H(e_M) < \pi_H(e_H)$ and costs $c_L < c_M < c_H$. Find first-best, show $e_M$ is not implementable, find optimal contracts for $e_L$ and $e_H$, pick the best.
                            • Step-by-step guide:
                            • First-best for each effort: constant wage $w^(e) = (\bar u + c(e))^2$. Principal picks $e$ to maximise $\pi_L(e) x_L + \pi_H(e) x_H - w^(e)$.
                            • Implementing $e_L$ under hidden action: same as A.6, constant wage $w(e_L) = (\bar u + c_L)^2$. No IC binding downward.
                            • Implementing $e_M$ under hidden action: this is the trick. To implement $e_M$ the principal must satisfy two IC constraints:
                            • IC vs $e_L$: $\pi_H^M u_H + \pi_L^M u_L - c_M \geq \pi_H^L u_H + \pi_L^L u_L - c_L$.
                            • IC vs $e_H$: $\pi_H^M u_H + \pi_L^M u_L - c_M \geq \pi_H^H u_H + \pi_L^H u_L - c_H$.

                            Rearranging using $u_H \neq u_L$:

                            $$(u_H - u_L)(\pi_H^M - \pi_H^L) \geq c_M - c_L, \qquad (u_H - u_L)(\pi_H^H - \pi_H^M) \leq c_H - c_M.$$

                            So we need $u_H - u_L$ to lie in an interval. The interval is non-empty iff the "marginal cost of effort per unit of probability shift" is increasing in effort, i.e. $(c_M - c_L)/(\pi_H^M - \pi_H^L) \leq (c_H - c_M)/(\pi_H^H - \pi_H^M)$. In 2017 Q10 the numbers are set so the interval is empty: no wage spread implements $e_M$ as the agent's choice.

                            1. Why $e_M$ is "skipped": the agent's preference over efforts is single-peaked given a wage schedule. Increasing the wage spread $u_H - u_L$ moves the agent's optimal effort from $e_L$ to $e_H$ directly without passing through $e_M$, because the cost-to-probability ratios make the middle effort dominated.
                            2. Implementing $e_H$: solve the binary-style problem from B.L.1, but with IC vs $e_L$ as the binding constraint (since $e_M$ is non-binding, we just need to beat $e_L$). Linear system:

                              3. $$\pi_L^H u_L + \pi_H^H u_H = \bar u + c_H, \qquad (\pi_H^H - \pi_H^L)(u_H - u_L) = c_H - c_L.$$

                              Solve for $u_L, u_H$, square to get $w_L, w_H$, compute $E[w^{SB}(e_H)]$.

                              1. Principal's best choice: compute net profit for $e_L$ (using constant wage) and $e_H$ (using second-best wage spread). The optimum is whichever gives higher profit.
                              2. Agency cost: difference between first-best profit at the chosen effort and second-best profit at the same effort.
                              3. Appeared in: 2017 Q10.
                              4. Relevant concepts: Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem, Concepts/Incentive Compatibility, Concepts/Monotone Likelihood Ratio, Concepts/Second-Best Contract, Concepts/Agency Cost.

                                5. Sub-question Type B.L.4: Three outcomes with wage bounds / limited liability (2024 Q10)

                                • What it asks: 2024 Q10 setup. Three success levels $H, M, L$ with wages $w_H, w_M, w_L$. Sqrt utility. Wage bounds either $w \geq -100$ or $w \geq 0$ depending on part. Find optimal contract under each regime, compare profits, agency cost, and the Pareto improvement from waiving the non-negativity right.
                                • Step-by-step guide:
                                • Low-effort baseline: constant wage $w^L = (\bar u + c_L)^2$ implementing $e_L$. Compute expected profit $\Pi(e_L) = \sum_s \pi_s^L x_s - w^L$.
                                • High-effort IR + IC without bounds: write IR and IC for implementing $e_H$ against $e_L$:

                                $$\sum_s \pi_s^H u_s - c_H \geq \bar u, \qquad \sum_s (\pi_s^H - \pi_s^L) u_s \geq c_H - c_L.$$

                                With three states this is two equations in three unknowns $(u_L, u_M, u_H)$. One degree of freedom remains and the principal exploits it.

                                1. Likelihood-ratio ordering: rank states by the likelihood ratio $\pi_s^L / \pi_s^H$. The state with the lowest ratio (most informative of high effort) should get the highest wage, the state with the highest ratio (most informative of low effort) should get the lowest wage.
                                2. In 2024 Q10, the middle outcome $M$ has the highest likelihood ratio (most informative that effort was $e_L$), so the principal wants $w_M$ as low as possible. Hence the corner solution.
                                3. Wage bound $w \geq -100$ regime: set $w_M = -100$ (the wage bound binds). With $w_M$ pinned down, the IR and IC are two equations in two remaining unknowns $(u_L, u_H)$. Solve.
                                4. Claim from the question: $w_H = w_L$. This holds when the likelihood ratios $\pi_H^L / \pi_H^H$ and $\pi_L^L / \pi_L^H$ are equal. In that case the principal is indifferent between paying in state $H$ vs state $L$, and pools them to share risk efficiently across those two non-punishing states.
                                5. With $w_H = w_L = w$, the IR becomes $(\pi_H^H + \pi_L^H) \sqrt{w} + \pi_M^H \sqrt{-100} - c_H \geq \bar u$. But $\sqrt{-100}$ is not real; in this question the punishment state contributes $-\infty$ in utility terms unless the agent never lands there. The correct reading is: the wage is $-100$ in state $M$ (a fine), and the agent's utility in that state is treated as $-\infty$ for the sqrt utility, OR the question uses a different utility specification at negative wages. Check the question's exact wording. (In 2024 Q10 the utility is interpreted so that very low wages give very low utility, making state $M$ the punishment state.)
                                6. Wage bound $w \geq 0$ regime: now $w_M = 0$ is the corner. Repeat the analysis: solve for $w_H, w_L$ from IR and IC. With $w_M = 0$ the "punishment" is weaker, so the spread between $w_H$ and $w_L$ must be larger to satisfy IC. Specifically, the IC determines $u_H - u_L$ from the high-vs-low states' likelihood ratios.
                                7. Compare profits: $\Pi$ under $w \geq -100$ is higher than under $w \geq 0$ (stronger punishment reduces wage bill). The "extra" cost under $w \geq 0$ is the limited liability rent.
                                8. Pareto improvement from waiving the non-negative wage right: the agent gives up the right to never be fined (state $M$ wage drops from $0$ to $-100$). In return the principal lowers expected total wage cost, but if the agent gets to renegotiate the wage upward to maintain IR with equality, both can be better off. The Pareto improvement is the area between the two regimes' agency costs.
                                9. Compute agency cost: $E[w^{SB}(e_H)] - w^{FB}(e_H)$ in each regime.
                                10. Appeared in: 2024 Q10.
                                11. Relevant concepts: Concepts/Principal-Agent Problem, Concepts/Wage Bounds Limited Liability, Concepts/Incentive Compatibility, Concepts/Individual Rationality, Concepts/Agency Cost, Concepts/Second-Best Contract.

                                  12. How to recognise the format on the day

                                  Trigger phrase in questionLikely sub-question type

                                  "effort is unobservable / hidden" + binary statesA.2 / B.L.1, solve IR + IC linear system in $\sqrt{w_L}, \sqrt{w_H}$
